# Connect a wallet
One press in the top bar. The site reads your address and your network, then stops.

Open gachiz.fun and press Connect in the top bar. A window lists the wallets found in your browser: pick yours, then approve in your wallet.

Close. The cross, the Escape key or a press outside closes the window. Nothing is shared.
Wallet found. One row per wallet your browser announces. Press yours.
Its state. Detected at rest. Waiting for your wallet while your wallet asks you to approve.

## Connect in four steps

Press Connect
The Connect button is at the right of the top bar, on the home page and in the demo.

Pick your wallet
Each wallet found in your browser has a row, tagged Detected. Press yours: the tag becomes Waiting for your wallet.

Approve in your wallet
Your wallet asks to share your address with the site. There is no payment and no signature to give.

Read the answer
An alert says Wallet connected, with the name of the wallet and a short form of your address. The button in the top bar now shows that short address.

## What the site reads

Your address. It is public: anyone can already see it on-chain.
Your network. The chain id your wallet is set to. If it is not Robinhood Chain, an alert says so: see Network.

## What it never asks

A transaction. The preview has none to send.
A signature. Nothing is signed when you connect.
A seed phrase or a private key. No page of Gachiz asks for one. A page that does is not Gachiz.

## Once connected

Press the short address in the top bar. A menu opens with your address, the name of your wallet and your network.

Copy address copies the full address. An alert confirms: Address copied.
Disconnect makes the site forget the address. An alert confirms: Wallet disconnected.

## What can go wrong

Connection cancelled in the wallet. | You closed or refused the request in your wallet. | Nothing was shared. Press your wallet again when ready. |
The wallet did not connect. Try again. | The wallet did not answer. | Open the wallet, unlock it, then press its row again. |
Your wallet is on another network | You are connected, on a network other than Robinhood Chain. | Switch, or stay: see Network. |

# Perpetuals
A contract that follows a price, with no end date. You hold it as long as your margin allows.

Start here if perps are new to you: five ideas carry the rest of these docs.

## The idea

A perpetual, or perp, is a position on a price move. You do not buy the cards. You take a side on the index: up or down. If the index moves your way, the position gains. If it moves against you, the position loses, and the loss comes out of what you put down.

## Five ideas

Side: Long gains when the index rises, short when it falls.

Margin: The amount locked behind a position. It absorbs the losses.

Leverage: How much larger the position is than its margin.

Liquidation: When losses reach the margin, the market closes the position.

Funding: A payment between longs and shorts while a position is open.

Index: The one number all of this is measured on.

## A perp, or the item itself

You hold a card
You own an object.
You gain only if its price rises.
You pay it in full.
Selling takes a buyer, and time.

You hold a perp
You own a position, not an object.
You can gain on a rise or on a fall.
You put down a margin, not the full size.
You close when you choose, or the market closes you.

## In what order to read

Long and short: pick a side and open a paper position.
Leverage and margin: size it.
Liquidation: know where it ends by itself.
Funding: know what holding it can cost.
Trade screen: try all of it with demo credits.

## What you do not get

No card and no pack. A position is a number on a market. It cannot be exchanged for an item, and it gives no right over any collectible.

A perp can cost the whole margin
Read Risks before any trade with funds. Today no funds are involved: the demo runs on credits with no value.

# Leverage and margin
Margin is what you put down. Leverage is how much larger the position is.

Set the margin first, then the leverage: together they fix the size of the position, and how far liquidation sits.

Slider. One step per unit, from 1× to 10×.
Presets. 1×, 2×, 5× and 10×. The one in use is lit.
Room before liquidation. At 10× it is down to 7.5%. At 5× it was 17.5%.
Value. Here 250 credits of margin at 10×: a value of 2,500.
Liquidation price. Liq. price, est. For a short, it sits above the entry.
Open. Orange for a short, with the market in the label.

## Margin

Margin is the amount locked behind a position. Losses are taken from it. When it runs out, the position is liquidated. In the demo it is counted in credits, and it leaves your available credits for as long as the position is open.

## Leverage

Position size equals margin times leverage.
A move of the index, in percent, applies to the size.
The gain or the loss lands on the margin.

So leverage multiplies the move as felt on your margin. At 5×, a 2% move of the index is a 10% move on your margin, up or down.

## Leverage and the distance to liquidation

With the demo settings, a position is liquidated when the index has moved against it by one over the leverage, minus 2.5%. The table takes an entry at 1,240.00 points.

Leverage | Move that liquidates | Liq. of a long | Liq. of a short |  |
1× | 97.5% | 31.00 pts | 2,449.00 pts | Demo setting |
2× | 47.5% | 651.00 pts | 1,829.00 pts | Demo setting |
5× | 17.5% | 1,023.00 pts | 1,457.00 pts | Demo setting |
10× | 7.5% | 1,147.00 pts | 1,333.00 pts | Demo setting |

More leverage, closer liquidation
At 1× the index must lose almost everything. At 10× a move of 7.5% is enough.

## The whole picture

One line per leverage. Each one falls faster than the one before, and stops at its liquidation point.

Margin left against the move of the index, at each leverage of the demo.

## Room before liquidation

The ticket shows that move as a bar, Room before liquidation, with its percent. Raise the leverage and the bar empties. The Positions tab then follows what is left of it, in the column To liq.

## How much margin fits

The demo takes no fee, so the largest margin is your available credits. Max fills the field with them. 10%, 25% and 50% take that share.

## What can go wrong

A margin too small. Under 10 credits, the ticket refuses the order.
A margin too large. The margin cannot exceed your available credits.
A size you did not mean. The size follows the leverage. Read Value before you press the button.

# Liquidation
When losses reach the margin, the market closes the position for you.

Find the liquidation price before you open: it is the level where the position closes by itself. The ticket shows it as Liq. price, est., the Positions tab as Liq.

## How it happens

The index moves against you
Down for a long, up for a short.

The margin shrinks
The loss so far is counted against the margin, even while the position is open.

The line is reached
The market closes the position at the liquidation price. You do not choose the moment.

## Read it on two positions

A long and a short do not read the same way. The liquidation level is on the far side of the entry from where you want the index to go.

Liquidation level. Liq. is fixed when the position opens. Above the entry for the short, below it for the long.
Entry, mark. The level at the open, and the level now.
Room left. To liq. is the room left, in percent of the index, with a bar that empties as the index nears the line.

## The formula of the demo

The demo fixes the liquidation price when the position opens, from the entry and the leverage.

Take one over the leverage, and subtract 2.5%, the maintenance margin of the demo.
For a long, lower the entry by that share. For a short, raise it.
That level is the liquidation price. It does not move afterwards.

Example
Entry at 1,240.00 points, 5×. One over 5 is 20%, minus 2.5%: 17.5%. A long is liquidated at 1,023.00 points, a short at 1,457.00 points.

## What you lose

In the demo, a liquidated position loses its whole margin, and nothing more: 500 credits in the example above. An alert says Liquidated with the market and the side, and the Trade history tab keeps the line in orange.

A close you choose is capped the same way: its result can never be worse than the margin.

## Stay away from the line

Use less leverage. The liquidation price moves further from the entry. See the table on Leverage and margin.
Watch the room. Read To liq. in the Positions tab: the room left, in percent. On the chart, the Liq. line shows when it is in range.
Close early. You choose the exit, not the market.

# Funding
A payment between longs and shorts that keeps the perp price close to the index.

Check the funding rate before you hold a position for long: it is paid or received for as long as the position is open.

## Who pays whom

The perp trades above the index
Longs pay shorts.
Being long costs a little more to hold.

The perp trades below the index
Shorts pay longs.
Being short costs a little more to hold.

## Why it exists

A perp has no end date, so nothing forces its price back to the index. Funding does: the crowded side pays the other one, which makes the crowded side less comfortable and the other side more.

## The cycle

The funding cycle of a perp, in general. Not Gachiz parameters.

## A worked example

General math, not Gachiz parameters. Funding is a rate applied to the position size, once per interval.

 | Long | Short |
Position size | 2,500.00 | 2,500.00 |
Rate for the interval | +0.01% | +0.01% |
Payment | pays 0.25 | receives 0.25 |

The payment follows the size, not the margin. With leverage, a small rate is larger once measured against what you put down.

## What to watch

The sign. It says which side pays.
The interval. A rate per hour and the same rate per day are not the same cost.

# Market, limit and stop
Three ways to open: now, at a better price you set, or once the index breaks a level. Plus a target and a stop loss.

Choose the order type under the side, on the ticket: Market opens now, Limit waits for a better price, Stop waits for the index to break a level.

## Three order types

Type | It opens | Use it to |
Market | Now, at the level of the index. | Get in at once. |
Limit | When the index reaches your price, at that price. | Wait for a better entry. |
Stop | When the index crosses your price, at the level of that second. | Follow a move once it has started. |

## Market

A market order opens the position at the level of the index when you press the button. The ticket calls that level Entry, est.: an estimate, because the index moves every second. The position takes the level of the second it opens.

## Limit

A limit order names a price, in points. The demo keeps the order until the index reaches it.

Limit. The second order type. A price field appears next to the margin.
Limit, pts. The level you want, in points. Below the index for a long, above it for a short.
Entry at limit. The estimates now start from your price, not from the current level.
Place. The button changes to Place limit long. The order then waits in Open orders.

Side | Where to set the limit | It fills when |
Long | Below the index | the index falls to your price, or under it. |
Short | Above the index | the index rises to your price, or over it. |

A limit on the wrong side does not wait
A long limit at or above the index, or a short limit at or below it, opens now at the current level. The button then reads Open, not Place limit.

## Stop

A stop order also names a price, in points, but it waits on the other side of the index: it opens once the index has moved through your price.

Side | Where to set the stop | It fills when |
Long | Above the index | the index rises to your price, or over it. |
Short | Below the index | the index falls to your price, or under it. |

A stop fills at the level of the second the index crosses it, which can be a little past your price. The ticket shows Entry at stop, est. and the button reads Place stop long. On the wrong side, the ticket refuses it: A long stop sits above the index.

## Reduce only

Turn on Reduce only to shrink a position instead of opening one. The order only closes positions of the other side on the market in view, the oldest first.

The field changes. It reads Value to reduce, credits: the value of the positions to close, not a margin.
Leverage is off. A reduce opens nothing, so it takes no leverage and no liquidation price.
The button changes. It reads Reduce long PKMN-IDX for a long that reduces a short.
It needs a position. With nothing to reduce, the ticket says so: Reduce only: no short position on PKMN-IDX.

## Target and stop loss

Turn on TP / SL to give a new position its exits, in points. Both fields are optional.

Field | For a long | For a short | When the index reaches it |
Target, pts | Above the entry | Below the entry | The position closes at the target. |
Stop loss, pts | Between the liquidation price and the entry | Between the entry and the liquidation price | The position closes at the level of that second. |

Both wait in Open orders with their position, and leave with it. On an open position, the TP / SL button of its row sets or changes them.

A stop loss sits before the liquidation
A stop loss past the liquidation price would never be reached: the ticket refuses it, and says where it has to sit.

## Set a limit from the book

The order book sits next to the chart, with the last trades under it. Press a price in it: the ticket switches to Limit, with that price in the field. The three buttons above the book group its levels into wider steps.

Grouping. Groups the levels of the book into wider steps.
Asks. Simulated sell levels above the index, with their size and running total.
Press a price. It fills the ticket with a limit at that price.
Level, spread. The index now, and the gap between the first ask and the first bid.
Bids. Simulated buy levels below the index.
Trades. The last simulated trades, newest first.
Simulated. The tag says it: no real order rests here.

## While it waits

It is listed. The Open orders tab shows it, and its count goes up.
Its margin is set aside. It leaves your available credits and counts as In use.
It is on the chart. A dashed grey line marks its price, when that price is in range.
It fills. A limit opens at its own price, a stop at the level of the second. An alert says Limit order filled or Stop order filled.

## The Open orders tab

Column | What it is |
Order | The index, the side and the leverage. |
Type | Limit, Stop, Target or Stop loss. |
Trigger, pts | The price it waits for. |

Margin held | What is set aside for it. |

## A worked example

With the demo settings. PKMN-IDX is at 1,240.00 points. You place a long limit at 1,235.04 points, 300 credits of margin, 5×.

Size: 1,500.00. Set aside while it waits: 300.00 credits.
The index falls to 1,235.04: the position opens there, not lower.
Its liquidation price is 1,018.91 points, computed from the limit.

## What can go wrong

Demo limit: 8 open orders at once. | Cancel one before you place another. |

A limit or a stop can also never fill: if the index does not reach your price, it waits until you cancel it, reset the demo or reload.

## How PnL is counted

Take the move of the index since the entry, in percent.
Apply it to the position size. For a short, flip the sign.
That is the PnL in credits. The second figure is the same PnL as a share of your margin.

Example
A long of 2,500.00 opened at 1,240.00 points, index now at 1,302.00: up 5%, so +125.00 credits, or +25% of a margin of 500.

Unrealized PnL. The result if you closed now. It moves with the index. The tab shows this one.
Realized PnL. The result once the position is closed. It no longer moves. The Trade history tab keeps it.

# Fees
What a perps market can charge, what the demo charges, and what Gachiz has announced: nothing yet.

Count the costs of any perps market before you trade on it. There are usually four.

## The usual costs

Trading fee. A share of the position size, charged when you open and when you close.
Funding. Paid or received while the position is open. See Funding.
Gas. The network fee of each transaction, in ETH on Robinhood Chain.
Liquidation fee. Many markets charge one when a position is liquidated.

## In the demo

Cost | In the demo |  |

## How a fee adds up

General math, not Gachiz parameters. A fee is a rate applied to the position size, not to the margin.

Margin 500 at 5×: a size of 2,500.00.
A rate of 0.05% on that size is 1.25 at the open, and about the same at the close.
The same margin at twice the leverage pays twice the fee.

# Risks
A perp can cost you the whole margin. Indexes of collectibles add risks of their own.

Read this page before any trade with funds: it lists what can go wrong, without softening it.

## Risks of any perp

You can lose the whole margin. A move against you is taken from it until nothing is left.
Leverage cuts both ways. It multiplies the losses as much as the gains. See the table on Leverage and margin.
Liquidation can be fast. A sharp move can close a position before you react. You do not choose the moment.
Funding adds up. Held for long, a position can pay more funding than expected.
Fees add up. Opening and closing often costs a fee each time, on the full size.

## Risks of collectibles

Few sales. Collectibles trade rarely. An index built on few sales can lag, then jump.
Hard to price. Two copies of the same item can sell far apart. The price source matters, and it is not announced yet.
Moved by taste. A card game can fall out of favour for reasons no chart shows.
Moved by its owner. A new set, a reprint or a change of rules from the owner of a license can move the prices of its cards.
Thin markets. A market with few traders can move a lot on one order.

## Risks of a preview

Parameters can change. Nothing marked Not announced yet is settled.
Software and networks can fail. A contract, a wallet or the network itself can break or stall.
Fakes appear. A new project attracts copies. See Spot a fake.

## Risks of a demo habit

Credits are not funds. Losing 500 demo credits teaches the math, not the feeling.
The index is simulated. It has no news, no crowd and no gaps. A real market has all three.
A reload forgives everything. A real position does not reset.

## Where each risk is explained

Risk | Page |

Being liquidated | Liquidation |
Paying to hold | Funding |
Paying to trade | Fees |
A fake site, a fake address | Spot a fake |

## What you can do

Size small. A position you can lose in full is the only kind to open.
Know the line. Read the liquidation price before you open, not after.
Read the page of each cost. Fees and Funding, once their parameters are announced.

## Market words

Index | One number that follows the sales of many cards. See Index price.

Point | The unit of an index level. A score, not a price.

Candle | The open, high, low and close of the index over one slice of time.

Perp | A perpetual: a position on a price move, with no end date. See Perpetuals.

Side | Long or short.

Long | The side that gains when the index rises.

Short | The side that gains when the index falls.

Funding | A payment between longs and shorts while a position is open. See Funding.

Mark price | The level used to value an open position. On the demo screen: Mark.

Collateral | The asset a margin is paid in. Not announced yet for Gachiz.

## Position words

Margin | The amount locked behind a position. See Leverage and margin.

Leverage | Position size divided by margin.

Position size | Margin times leverage. Fees and moves apply to it.

Entry | The index level when a position opened.

Liq. | The liquidation price: the level where the market closes the position. See Liquidation.

To liq. | The room left before the liquidation price, in percent of the index.

Maintenance margin | The share of the size a position must keep before it is liquidated.

PnL | The gain or the loss of a position. Unrealized while open, realized once closed.

Open PnL | The PnL of all open positions together.

Close | End a position at the current level. See Positions.

## Order words

Market order | An order that opens now, at the current level. See Market and limit.

Limit order | An order that waits for a price you set.

Resting order | A limit order that has not filled yet. Listed in the Orders tab.

Fill | The moment a waiting order becomes a position.

Paper order | An order kept by the demo and never sent to a chain. See Trade screen.

## Demo and wallet words

Credits | The simulated unit of the demo. No value, reset at each reload.

Equity | Available plus in use plus open PnL.

Available | The credits a new order can use.

In use | Margins of open positions, and of limit orders that wait.

Book | The order book: levels where orders rest. In the demo it is drawn from the simulated index.

Spread | The gap between the first ask and the first bid.

Simulated index | An index computed in your browser. It does not follow real sales.

Chain id | The number that names a network. 4663 for Robinhood Chain. See Network.

Gas | The network fee of a transaction.

Contract address | The address of a token on a network. The GACHIZ token has none yet. See Status.
